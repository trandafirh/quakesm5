From the seismometer to your wallet

Four steps, every day at a random hour between 09:00 and 20:00 UTC.

  1. 1

    Three catalogs count

    USGS, EMSC and GEOFON each count the magnitude 5+ earthquakes of the last 30 days. Every Opsikom node takes the median of the three.

  2. 2

    Publish and update, together

    Independent nodes sign the value. The Opsikom relayer publishes the round and updates MW5 in the same transaction, so nobody has to remember to call update(). It stays open to anyone as a backup.

  3. 3

    A slow 90-day average

    The new value joins a 90-day moving average. A soft curve turns the average into a level, and the level moves at most 0.7% per update, at most once every 20 hours.

  4. 4

    Burn from the pool, or mint to you

    Level down (more quakes): that share of the V2 pool's MW5 is burned, so the price goes up. Level up (fewer quakes): holders receive new MW5, 99.3% to them and 0.7% to liquidity.

Your balance never goes down. A burn only touches the Uniswap V2 pool, never a wallet, and it pushes the price up. So there is no reason to sell before a burn. The catalogs are public, which is why the level moves slowly: nobody gains much by guessing tomorrow's number.

Level = 1 ± 0.3 × d / (70 + d), where d is the distance of the 90-day average from the launch value 189 (minus when there are more quakes, plus when there are fewer), with daily values capped at 400. The level moves at most 0.7% per update. The simulator starts with all 90 days at the average you enter and the level already on the curve. A level step up mints that share to holders (99.3% to you, 0.7% to liquidity); a step down burns that share of the V2 pool and never touches your wallet. The pool price shown is the V2 spot price with nothing else moving. Over the 2006–2025 data the level stays between ×0.84 and ×1.16; in a month it moves about 3% on average, never more than about +23% or −18.5%. An estimate, not financial advice.

Two shares. Nothing for the team.

At launch the contract computes a "historical burn": the share the old curve would have burned going from zero earthquakes to the real value of the feed on launch day. With 189 quakes that is 27.96% of the supply. Those coins are not destroyed: they go to the community, as a 90-day airdrop and an on-chain faucet.

Liquidity pools

5,042,457,791 MW5

80% in a Uniswap-style V2 pool, 20% in a V3 pool. LP tokens and the V3 position go to the dead address: the liquidity is locked forever. Burns use the V2 pool only.

Airdrop and faucet

1,957,542,209 MW5

80% unlocks a little every day for 90 days from launch: anyone can call releaseAirdrop() to send the unlocked part to the airdrop wallet. 20% is an on-chain faucet: 5,000 MW5 per wallet per day, up to 500 claims a day, for wallets holding at least 0.0005 ETH.

Launch day decides

25% – 35%

The share depends on the feed value on launch day. The contract refuses to deploy if the community share falls outside 25–35%.

0.7%, and all of it goes to liquidity

No team wallet and no tax for anybody. Every fee is collected by the contract and added to the V2 pool, with the LP tokens burned.

Why sells are free. A pool expects to receive exactly the amount it is sold. A fee on sells would make V2 swaps fail and V3 refuse the payment: MW5 could not be sold at all.
